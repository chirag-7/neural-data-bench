# Independent coverage audit of the neuroscience benchmark map

Audit date: 2026-10-30. Input: `outputs/neuroscience_benchmark_catalog.json`, 90 task–resource pairings in 14 families. This is a bounded gap audit, not a systematic literature review or a new validation of every existing entry. Resources below were checked through primary papers/project documentation; downloads, license review, installation, scoring and current-best searches were not performed here.

## Verdict

The map covers the major modalities of contemporary data-intensive experimental neuroscience unusually broadly: behavior, electrophysiology, optical physiology, population dynamics, BCI, EEG/MEG, MRI, anatomy, molecular data, inference and agent workflows. Its weakness is that **breadth of modalities does not establish completeness of scientific operations**. A reviewer could reasonably identify gaps in intervention prediction, explanatory-model validation, cognitive-model recovery, embodied control, source inversion and diffusion tractography. Several common lab operations, especially QC and artifact rejection, also deserve explicit endpoints rather than being implied by a workflow benchmark.

The correct claim is **a broad, versioned evidence map with explicit scope and known omissions**. “All of computational neuroscience,” “no glaring gaps,” and “systematic review” are not yet defensible. Existing entries often combine several scientifically distinct endpoints; 90 rows does not mean 90 independent benchmarks or 90 execution-ready tasks.

## Nine substantive gaps or underdeveloped perspectives

The scope labels below are recommendations. “Core” means needed for a broad claim about neuroscience laboratory analysis and computational modeling; “extension” means worthwhile but should not delay an executable first release.

| Priority and scope | Missing/underdeveloped operation | Why the current entry is insufficient | Concrete resource and proposed benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Core | **Intervention-response prediction and causal identification** | L01 functional-connectivity inference and L03 null testing do not test responses to controlled perturbations. Good observational prediction does not establish intervention prediction. | The **C. elegans neural signal-propagation atlas** measures neural responses to optogenetic perturbations, with machine-readable measurements and analysis code described by the authors. Proposed endpoint: held-out perturbation response, sign/strength/time course, uncertainty and intervention generalization. Split by animal/experiment and perturbation target where scientifically possible. This is **data requiring a wrapper**, not a confirmed ready-made leaderboard. [Paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06683-4), [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.04790), [data DOI](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/E2SYT). |
| 2 — Core for theoretical labs | **Validate explanatory biophysical models against multiple independent protocols** | D02 current-to-voltage and L02 parameter inference only partly cover whether an inferred mechanism survives independent physiological tests. | **HippoUnit/SciUnit** already separates model execution from test/score definitions. Somatic features, depolarization block, backpropagating spikes, PSP attenuation and dendritic integration are explicit tests. An agent could construct/adapt a model; score it on held-out protocols and feature errors, not prose plausibility. Integrate with published NEURON models before attempting broad simulation tasks. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008114), [test documentation](https://hippounit.readthedocs.io/page_tests.html). |
| 3 — Core for computational/cognitive labs | **Cognitive model recovery, parameter identifiability and model checking** | M01 includes behavioral fitting, but predictive choice accuracy alone cannot tell whether a model recovers the correct latent process. | Wilson and Collins provide example code and explicit model/parameter-recovery guidance. Add synthetic bandit/drift-diffusion/reinforcement-learning cases with known generating parameters; score parameter recovery, model-confusion matrix, held-out likelihood and posterior predictive checks. **BADS CCN17** provides published real model-fitting optimization problems from six neuroscience studies; use it as a fitting-efficiency reference after checking reusable release/access. Neither is a new “current best” claim. [Modeling guide](https://elifesciences.org/articles/49547), [BADS primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.04405). |
| 4 — Core family, heavier extension to execution | **Embodied neuromechanics and feedback motor control** | M03 NeuroGym is useful for cognitive task environments, but it is not a substitute for musculoskeletal dynamics, sensory feedback, energetic cost or physical perturbations. | **MyoChallenge/MyoSuite** supplies standardized muscle-actuated tasks and competitions; **FlyGym/NeuroMechFly v 2** adds animal embodiment, vision, olfaction, proprioception and terrain. Keep task return/success, stability, energy and robustness separate. A general agent writing/training a policy is a different track from a direct policy. Freeze environment versions and simulation budgets. [MyoChallenge](https://myosuite.org/myochallenge/), [2024 benchmark paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/5a8f69523f9511a5706568c552de0ebb-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html), [FlyGym paper/data/code availability](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02497-y). |
| 5 — Core if EEG/MEG is retained | **Forward/inverse modeling and source localization** | EEG/MEG classification, decoding and multimodal association do not cover localization of the neural generators or correct head/sensor geometry. | **MNE/Brainstorm phantom datasets** provide known dipole locations and reusable localization tutorials. Score location/orientation error, source recovery and uncertainty under noise/co-registration perturbations. Distinguish physical-phantom truth, simulated truth and uncertain in-vivo localization. Do not label a phantom result as human-brain source accuracy. [MNE Elekta phantom protocol](https://mne.tools/stable/auto_tutorials/inverse/80_brainstorm_phantom_elekta.html), [inverse-model task overview](https://mne.tools/1.5/auto_tutorials/inverse/index.html). |
| 6 — Extension; essential for an “all neuroimaging” claim | **Diffusion MRI microstructure/tractography and structural connectivity** | I05 MRI reconstruction, I06 lesions and J01 EM segmentation leave out a major distinct structural-neuroimaging workflow. | **ISMRM 2015/Tractometer** has diffusion phantom data, scoring data and scripts. Use valid/invalid connections, bundle overlap and overreach. Crucially, **2015 and 2023 scoring differ**; the updated protocol uses 21 bundles rather than the old 25. Scores across those versions are not directly interchangeable. [Official scoring/version notes](https://tractometer.org/ismrm2015/tools/), [challenge submissions archive](https://zenodo.org/records/840086). |
| 7 — Core, cross-modal | **QC, artifact discrimination and repair as first-class outcomes** | Optical denoising and generic N05 data loading do not explicitly cover bad channels, bad trials, EEG ocular/muscle artifacts, MRI accept/reject decisions, synchronization errors or metadata integrity. | **EEGdenoiseNet** provides clean/ocular/muscle segments for semi-synthetic contamination and denoising metrics. Its reference is curated signal, not pristine biological truth. **MRIQC** provides expert quality ratings and out-of-site evaluation. Proposed additional QC cases: wrong sampling-rate units, channel swaps and timestamp offsets inserted into a valid NWB/BIDS file, scored by fault localization and retained scientific result. Synthetic corruption tasks must be labeled proposed. [EEGdenoiseNet primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/ac2bf8), [MRIQC primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0184661), [MRIQC QC tutorial](https://www.nipreps.org/qc-book/auto-qc/iqms_first_contact.html). |
| 8 — Core reporting axis; neuromorphic methods an extension | **Online constraints, compute/latency/energy and continual adaptation** | Offline accuracy can obscure unusable latency for BCI/closed-loop experiments and very unequal cost between specialists and general agents. Adopt the reporting principles on neuroscience tasks: wall-clock latency distribution, throughput, memory, call/compute cost, training budget and failure rate. Do not include generic speech/event-camera tasks as neuroscience simply because an SNN solves them. [Primary framework paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-56739-4), [harness documentation](https://neurobench.readthedocs.io/en/latest/). |
| 9 — Core evidence standard | **Systematic search provenance and score comparability** | A curated catalog is not a documented search/screening process. “Best” can change with split, target preprocessing, supervision, scorer and cost. Some rows combine named benchmarks, tools and proposed tasks. | Follow **PRISMA-ScR/PRISMA-S** reporting elements for search sources, exact queries, dates, screening decisions and a flow diagram. Add benchmark cards separating official test results, published reimplementations, local reproductions and proposed pilot results. Neuroimaging benchmark recommendations and empirical leakage research are particularly relevant. [PRISMA-ScR](https://www.prisma-statement.org/scoping), [PRISMA-S](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8270366/), [neuroimaging benchmark recommendations](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119298), [leakage study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-46150-w). |

## Useful prior benchmark work beyond the map's obvious neighbors

**HippoUnit** is important prior art for model validation rather than predictive ML alone. **BADS/CCN17** reflects practical computational-neuroscience model fitting. **sbibm** supplies reference posteriors, metrics and evaluation tooling, useful for auditing L02; select neuroscience-linked tasks such as GLMs or a stated biophysical extension, rather than counting every generic inference toy as neuroscience. **NeuroBench** illustrates efficiency-aware comparison. **Tractometer** documents why scorer versions and biological ground-truth limitations matter. These complement the existing NLB, BrainWideBench, NeuralBench, SpikeForest, MOABB, NeuroDiscoveryBench and Brain-Score perspectives rather than replacing them. [sbibm repository and results links](https://github.com/sbi-benchmark/sbibm).

## A defensible taxonomy and inclusion rule

Keep the existing 14 families as a navigational view, but tag every endpoint along independent axes:

1. **Scientific operation:** acquire/QC, reconstruct, detect/segment, encode/decode, forecast/generate, infer parameters/latents/connectivity, test hypotheses, predict interventions, design/control experiments, reproduce a scientific workflow.
2. **Data and preparation:** modality, raw versus processed, species, body/brain scale, lab/clinical/naturalistic context, in-vivo/ex-vivo/synthetic/phantom, healthy/disease/developmental state.
3. **Ground truth:** simultaneous measurement, expert labels and disagreement, physical/simulated known truth, future observation, proxy target, or no identifiable truth.
4. **Transfer:** within session, new session/animal/person/lab/species/device/paradigm; zero/few/many labels; temporal distribution shift.
5. **Evaluation track:** direct general model, frozen features + readout, bounded adaptation, general-code agent, neuroscience-tool agent, neuroscience-pretrained model, specialist algorithm.

Include a resource in the **executable benchmark** set only when there is a neuroscience-relevant input/output operation, an available/licensed evaluation slice, a specified split and label-access policy, an executable scorer, and at least one reproducible baseline or a transparent reason it is absent. Keep **dataset requiring wrapper**, **tool requiring task construction**, **restricted/unverified release** and **proposed simulator task** as separate statuses. A resource can remain in the research map without entering the executable leaderboard.

Exclude generic medical imaging, generic machine-learning tasks and literature trivia unless a specific lab workflow or neuroscientific modeling rationale is stated. Clinical diagnosis/prognosis, aging/developmental normative modeling, psychiatric cohort prediction, neurochemical sensing, animal vocalization, autonomic physiology, spatial molecular assays and invasive intervention control should be documented as **extension boundaries**, not silently implied by a single MRI/EEG/molecular entry. They are legitimate perspectives, but this audit does not certify their coverage.

## What is required before paper-level completeness claims

The immediate feasible objective is an **auditable representative suite**, not universal coverage. For a paper, register the scope/search protocol; search neuroscience and ML venues plus benchmark/data portals; deduplicate underlying datasets and overlapping subjects; screen against the stated rule; log exclusions; and obtain expert review across experimental systems, human neuroimaging, behavior and theory. Archive the evidence table and allow versioned additions. Report coverage as a matrix of operations and modalities with intentional blanks, not a promise that no one can name another task.

For “current best,” store the exact endpoint, split, scorer version, original metric direction, data regime, pretrained-data disclosure, resource budget, source table/leaderboard, retrieval date and uncertainty. Keep **best published comparable**, **reproduced specialist baseline**, and **local pilot** separate. Never fill missing model scores with estimated values or normalize incompatible task metrics into an unqualified overall ranking. Empirical claims about progress over model generations require the same frozen cases, harness, prompt/tool policy and budgets.

## Verification limits

Primary documentation supports the existence and relevant operations of the resources above. A paper's data-availability statement is evidence of a release pathway, not proof that every link still downloads or that all assets can be redistributed. A few publisher pages were inaccessible to the browser; author preprints or project documentation were used where available. No new scores or model experiments are reported in this audit.
