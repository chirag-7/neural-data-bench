# Specialist review for measured task families A–G

Reviewed on **1 November 2026**. This review covers the 14 measured endpoints A09, A14, A15, B01, B03, B07, B08, C05, C06, D02, E01, E06, F04 and G01. Each accompanying JSON records fresh retrieval queries, primary sources, publication dates where verified, access limitations, candidate roles and the exact local protocol fingerprint. An access date is not a publication date, and a missing date means it was not verified.

**No current global best is verified on these exact custom protocols.** Twelve endpoints have strong candidates without a matched winner; B08 and D02 remain unresolved as current-best searches. This is not a claim that no best published result exists on any original benchmark. Published winners depend on data, input modality, split, target, calibration access and resources. Our results compare a bounded general-program pilot with particular local references or archived predictions. They do not yet justify switching away from established neuroscience tools.

The search included original papers, official code and current project/leaderboard pages, plus recent contenders rather than only the incumbent local reference. No training, large checkpoint downloads, model generation or new method scoring was performed. No published score was inserted into our results. Source access failures and dynamic leaderboards are recorded explicitly rather than filled in from memory.

## Findings that change comparator selection

Calcium inference needs an update beyond the historical Spikefinder archive. CASCADE remains important, and CascadeTorch is a framework port with converted weights rather than proof of improved accuracy. ENS2 is an established supervised contender. The **October 2026 SpikeSSL preprint** is especially relevant to the OGB/DS04 source but needs an exact specimen and training-source audit. C-SPIKES is a 2026 preprint with indicator-specific kinetics; PGBAR pursues posterior inference, and slugFind supplies another direct Spikefinder comparison. GCaMP8 results do not establish the best method for OGB1 zebrafish. The two calcium JSONs preserve these distinctions and link papers and code.

The motion comparison should distinguish current official DREDge from older decentralized implementations. The official repository now directs users to SpikeInterface `dredge`/`dredge_fast` presets; MEDiCINe is a substantive alternative with a different smoothing and detected-peak treatment. A synthetic count-raster result cannot establish the best full extracellular preprocessing pipeline.

The current neural-forecasting landscape includes POCO and iterative AI-generated software systems, not only NLinear. ZAPBench's original 4- or 256-frame, full-brain MAE tasks differ from our 64-frame, selected-neuron MSE task. POCO's paper reports different conclusions for short and long contexts. ERA and SimpleTES should be represented as resource-dependent software-search procedures, not silently grouped with one no-tool program generation or conventional specialist fitting.

For sorting and decoding, attractive recent reports still need access parity. Kilosort4's strong hybrid evidence does not establish superiority on every probe or short synthetic fixture; DARTsort explicitly remains research software. NDT3 pretrained source exposure must be checked before any custom FALCON date split. SPINT and APST access target calibration data that G01 forbids. Their published results cannot be copied into this pilot or used to rank its methods.

## Evidence limits and switching decision

The fresh search did not yield independently readable current numerical rows for the NLB or FALCON dynamic EvalAI leaderboards. NLB's challenge is closed and points to a January 2026 snapshot. The original Spikefinder challenge ran in 2016–2017. The Spikepack container benchmark failed direct retrieval twice. Some recent primary papers exposed indexed text but blocked direct full-text access; each JSON identifies that limitation. These are retrieval limits, not evidence that the unobserved contenders perform poorly.

Before a replacement recommendation, freeze a prospective comparison with identical data access, biological/source splits, preprocessing, targets and aggregation; audit pretrained overlap; select tuning settings on training data; and compare runtime, fidelity, calibration and failure behavior. Replicate across independent animals, sessions and data families. Preserve the original pilot and any repaired harness revisions separately. Additional modality, target adaptation or iterative search can be scientifically useful, but it should receive its own declared protocol and resource tier.

The accompanying JSONs contain candidate-specific negative findings and source dates. They make no formal exhaustiveness claim and no global-best claim unsupported by an official matched protocol.
